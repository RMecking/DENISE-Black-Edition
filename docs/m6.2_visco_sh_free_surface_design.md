# M6.2a viscoelastic SH free-surface discrete audit and verification design

## A. Scope and immutable provenance

This document is the pre-implementation design for an isotropic viscoelastic
SH free surface at the flat, grid-aligned plane `y=0`. It covers the existing
GSLS/Q forward rheology only. It does not change or validate viscoelastic SH
FWI/adjoints, attenuation inversion, topography, anisotropy, or optimizer
convergence.

The audit was performed on:

```text
repository: DENISE-Black-Edition
base branch: modernization
base commit: f0fad66c2521951d26ca40acf0460fed86e43eca
audit branch: codex/m6.2-visco-sh-free-surface-audit
```

M6.1's elastic implementation and locked tests are inputs to this design and
are not modified. The historical DENISE-SH snapshot is considered only in
section O, after the independent derivation, and is not an oracle.

## B. Active viscoelastic SH path at the base commit

### B.1 Entry and material preparation

For global `MODE=0`, `physics_SH()` calls `FD_SH()`. With `L>0`, that path:

1. reads `rho`, `Vs`/`mu`, and `Qs` with `readmod_visc_SH()`;
2. converts Qs through the current `q_to_tau()` mapping;
3. exchanges the material edge values with `matcopy_SH()`;
4. computes harmonic `mu` values and inverse density;
5. averages the x-shear relaxation field with `av_tau()`;
6. prepares the GSLS coefficients with `prepare_update_s_visc_SH()`; and
7. calls `sh_visc(..., mode=0)`.

The physical-Q path is selected by `Q_PARAMETERIZATION_MODE=1` and uses the
configured approximation band. This design preserves that path. It neither
uses nor derives `tau=2/Qs` as a replacement mapping.

Global `MODE=1` calls `FWI_SH_visc()` when `L>0`; its forward, adjoint, and
line-search calls reach the same `sh_visc()` timestep with lower-case modes 0,
1, and 2. Global `MODE=2` has no active SH RTM dispatch. Global `MODE=3` is the
legacy finite-difference gradient driver and is outside M6.2's acceptance
claim.

### B.2 Per-timestep sequence and lower-case mode

After all physical and ghost wave/memory entries are zeroed, every lower-case
mode uses this same leapfrog sequence:

```text
update_v_PML_SH
exchange_v_SH
update_s_visc_PML_SH
exchange_s_SH
receiver/snapshot and mode-specific storage
```

The mode differences are:

| `mode` | velocity source | sign/state use | purpose |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 0 | physical shot sources/signals | `ADJ_SIGN=+1`; may store forward states when global `MODE` requests them | forward/STF/FWI forward |
| 1 | receiver positions and reversed residual traces | `ADJ_SIGN=-1`; performs legacy viscoelastic gradient correlations | adjoint; explicitly outside M6.2 correctness |
| 2 | physical shot sources/signals | `ADJ_SIGN=+1`; no forward-state storage | line-search objective trial |

All three modes currently have identical `FREE_SURF` behavior. The upper-y
CPML terms in both velocity and stress updates are disabled when
`FREE_SURF=1`, but `sh_visc.c` does not call the M6.1 elastic SH helpers or any
viscoelastic SH surface routine. Its visible surface block is commented and
refers to PSV structures. Thus no active statement completes velocity parity,
sets total `syz[0]`, or mirrors total `syz` ghosts.

### B.3 Updated, exchanged, and disconnected fields

`update_v_PML_SH()` advances `vz` from backward staggered derivatives of
`sxz` and `syz`. `exchange_v_SH()` exchanges only `vz`. The viscoelastic stress
kernel advances active rows `j=1..NY` of `sxz`, `syz`, `r`, and `q`.
`exchange_s_SH()` exchanges only `sxz` and `syz`.

No routine exchanges `r`, `p`, or `q`. That is sufficient at internal MPI
boundaries because each memory recurrence is point-local: only velocity and
stress stencils consume neighbor halos. `p` is allocated and zeroed but is
never read or updated by the active SH constitutive kernel. It therefore has no
operational staggered location and must not be assigned one from its name.

## C. Staggered locations and initialized state

Let `h=DH`. The positions follow from the update equations and coefficient
indices:

| quantity | operational location | evidence/use |
|---|---|---|
| `vz[j][i]` | `((i-1/2)h,(j-1/2)h)` | cell-centered velocity update |
| `sxz[j][i]` | `(ih,(j-1/2)h)` | driven by `D+x vz`; consumed by `D-x sxz` |
| `syz[j][i]` | `((i-1/2)h,jh)` | driven by `D+y vz`; consumed by `D-y syz` |
| `r[j][i][l]` | with `sxz` | same `vzx` driver and same local update index as `sxz` |
| `q[j][i][l]` | with `syz` | same `vzy` driver and same local update index as `syz` |
| `p[j][i][l]` | none in active SH | allocated/zeroed, but absent from the constitutive equations |

The wave and memory tensors are allocated from row/column `-M` through the
positive halo, with `M=FDORDER/2`. `zero_denise_visc_SH()` initializes every
physical and ghost entry of `vz`, both stresses, and all `r/p/q` mechanisms to
zero. Only active `j=1..NY` entries of `r` and `q` are subsequently updated.
Their allocated ghosts remain zero and are never consumed or exchanged in the
base algorithm.

The physical surface remains the M6.1 location:

```text
y =  h/2    vz[1], first active material cell
y =  0      syz[0], q[0][l], physical total-traction/state row
y = -h/2    vz[0], velocity ghost
y = -h      syz[-1], q[-1][l], full-state ghosts
```

## D. Continuous viscoelastic traction and parity

For antiplane displacement `u=(0,0,u_z)`, an isotropic linear viscoelastic
solid has shear strains proportional to `partial_x u_z` and
`partial_y u_z`. Each stress is a causal linear constitutive response to its
corresponding strain history. At a horizontal top with outward normal
`n=(0,-1,0)`, the SH traction is

```text
t_z = -sigma_yz.
```

The physical boundary condition is on the **total** constitutive stress:

```text
sigma_yz(x,0,t) = 0.
```

It is not a condition on only the relaxed or instantaneous elastic term. The
traction-free image extension is

```text
u_z, vz             even in y
partial_y u_z       odd in y
sigma_yz            odd in y
partial_x u_z       even in y
sigma_xz            even in y.
```

Consequently, each local GSLS state driven by `partial_y vz` is odd, while
each state driven by `partial_x vz` is even. In the active code this means

```text
q_l[0][i] = 0
q_l[-k][i] = -q_l[k][i]
r_l[1-k][i] = +r_l[k][i]
```

for a full image state. `q_l[0]=0` is a constitutive-state closure implied by
even velocity, zero prehistory, and the implemented local recurrence. The
physical boundary condition itself remains only total `syz[0]=0`. The `q`
ghosts and even `r` ghosts are not required by any active forward stencil.

## E. Implemented GSLS surface recurrence

For either shear component, the kernel uses a trapezoidal split. For the
y-shear component at one grid point, define

```text
gamma = D+y vz
F     = mu_relaxed * DT * (1 + L*tau_s)
d_l   = mu_relaxed * eta_l * tau_s
b_l   = 1 / (1 + eta_l/2)
c_l   = 1 - eta_l/2.
```

The exact implemented sequence is

```text
Q_old = sum_l q_l^n
syz*  = syz^n + F*gamma + (DT/2)*Q_old
q_l^(n+1) = b_l * (c_l*q_l^n - d_l*gamma)
Q_new = sum_l q_l^(n+1)
syz^(n+1) = syz* + (DT/2)*Q_new.
```

The x-shear recurrence is identical in form with `r`, `vzx`, `fipjp`,
`dip`, `bip`, and `cip`.

With the even M6.1 velocity extension,

```text
(D+y vz)[0]
  = sum(m=1..M) hc[m] * (vz[m] - vz[1-m]) / DH
  = 0
```

coefficient by coefficient. Assume `syz^n[0]=0` and every
`q_l^n[0]=0`. Then for every mechanism independently,

```text
q_l^(n+1)[0] = b_l * (c_l*0 - d_l*0) = 0,
```

and therefore

```text
syz^(n+1)[0] = 0 + F*0 + (DT/2)*(0+0) = 0.
```

This proof does not use a Q value, a relaxation frequency, a sign or magnitude
restriction on the prepared coefficients, or `L=1`. It holds term by term for
every supported `L>=1`. It also shows why merely zeroing total stress while
allowing an inconsistent nonzero surface `q` would not define a closed
constitutive boundary state.

The current kernel does not advance row zero, so its initially zero `q[0]`
already remains zero. A future implementation decision must distinguish this
minimum forward state from explicitly materializing the full diagnostic image
state.

## F. Reuse of the M6.1 state and exact ghost requirements

The M6.1 velocity extension and total-stress traction/parity are
rheology-independent:

```text
vz[1-k][i]  =  vz[k][i],       k=1..M
syz[0][i]   =  0
syz[-k][i]  = -syz[k][i],      k=1..M-1.
```

For full constitutive-state diagnostics, add

```text
q_l[0][i]  = 0
q_l[-k][i] = -q_l[k][i],       k=1..M-1.
```

No production helper is selected in M6.2a. The minimum/full requirements are:

| FDORDER | M | active `vz` minimum | full `vz` | total `syz` | minimum `q` ghosts | full `q` state |
|---:|---:|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 1 | none | `0` | `0` | none | `0` |
| 4 | 2 | `0` | `-1,0` | `-1,0` | none | `-1,0` |
| 6 | 3 | `-1,0` | `-2,-1,0` | `-2,-1,0` | none | `-2,-1,0` |
| 8 | 4 | `-2,-1,0` | `-3,-2,-1,0` | `-3,-2,-1,0` | none | `-3,-2,-1,0` |
| 10 | 5 | `-3,-2,-1,0` | `-4,-3,-2,-1,0` | `-4,-3,-2,-1,0` | none | `-4,-3,-2,-1,0` |
| 12 | 6 | `-4,-3,-2,-1,0` | `-5,-4,-3,-2,-1,0` | `-5,-4,-3,-2,-1,0` | none | `-5,-4,-3,-2,-1,0` |

`r` needs no top ghost for the active algorithm because it is point-local and
no y-normal stress stencil consumes it. Its even parity remains a pure/full
state diagnostic. `p` has neither a minimum nor a full-state requirement until
an operational SH equation gives it a meaning.

## G. MPI ownership and candidate ordering

`POS[2]==0` identifies all top ranks. Their row-zero and negative-y slots are
physical-boundary state. For `POS[2]>0`, the corresponding velocity/stress
slots are internal-y MPI halos and must never be overwritten by a surface
operation.

The independently derived candidate order is:

1. `update_v_PML_SH` consumes the completed total-`syz` state from the prior
   stress phase;
2. `exchange_v_SH` fills internal MPI velocity halos;
3. top ranks with `FREE_SURF=1` complete the full even `vz` extension;
4. `update_s_visc_PML_SH` updates active `sxz/syz/r/q` rows;
5. top ranks complete total `syz[0]` and its odd ghosts;
6. if full constitutive diagnostics are selected, the same top ranks set
   `q_l[0]=0` and fill odd `q_l` ghosts after the active recurrence;
7. `exchange_s_SH` fills internal stress halos.

Steps 5 and 6 are independent local state completions and can share a helper,
but M6.2a does not choose that API. `q` has no MPI halo ownership issue because
no neighbor stencil reads it and it is not exchanged. Non-top ranks retain
their independently evolved active `q` cells. At a 2x1 decomposition each top
x-rank completes its local surface columns; at 1x2 only y-rank zero does so.

Upper CPML remains excluded when `FREE_SURF=1`. Lateral x CPML remains active,
including at the top corners, and must not be overwritten. Bottom CPML remains
active. Surface completion must touch neither x halos nor CPML memory.

## H. Independent viscoelastic image-source oracle

The primary black-box oracle compares two problems that use the same current
rheology and never call production surface code to create the reference.

### H.1 Candidate half-space

Problem A has a source and receivers on native `vz` points below `y=0`, uses
`FREE_SURF=1`, and contains one physical source. Native positions are derived
from staggered indices, not raw rounded input coordinates.

### H.2 Translated full-space reference

Choose an interior reference plane `Y` on a native `syz` row, so `Y/h` is an
integer. If the candidate native points are

```text
S=(xs,ys), R=(xr,yr), ys>0, yr>0,
```

problem B uses

```text
S_real  = (xs,Y+ys)
S_image = (xs,Y-ys)
R_ref   = (xr,Y+yr).
```

Both sources have the same SH polarity and identical wavelet. Integer-cell
translation preserves native half-cell `vz` phase, and

```text
|R_ref-S_real|  = |R-S|
|R_ref-S_image| = |R-(xs,-ys)|.
```

Problem B must be large enough that no outer CPML return enters the complete
direct-plus-reflected comparison window. A and B use identical `L`, relaxation
frequencies, Q mapping/band, Qs, FDORDER, DH, DT, source wavelet, and receiver
component. All samples in the predeclared reflected/image window are compared;
the oracle is not a single pick and the interval is not broadened after seeing
the candidate.

Metrics to freeze after geometry/sensitivity validation are normalized
correlation, relative L2, signed peak/amplitude ratio, and sub-sample or
sample-resolved arrival lag. The direction of acceptance is predeclared:
correlation tends to one, relative L2 and lag tend to zero, and the signed
amplitude ratio tends to one. M6.2a does not assign numerical limits from
unobserved production data. Limits must later be justified from sampling,
dispersion, finite-domain contamination, and roundoff before the candidate
repair is run.

`FREE_SURF=0` is an independent negative/control run demonstrating that no
comparable top reflection exists. It is not subtracted from the primary image
waveform.

## I. High-Q elastic convergence

Under the current physical-Q mapping, increasing target Q drives the fitted
`tau_s` toward zero. Then the prepared relaxed modulus tends to the elastic
reference modulus, `d_l` tends to zero, all memory responses vanish, and `F`
tends to `mu*DT`. Therefore the complete viscoelastic `FREE_SURF=1` waveform
must converge to the already verified elastic M6.1 waveform for identical
geometry, FDORDER, DH, DT, and wavelet.

The convergence study should reuse the established `(50, 200, 1000)` high-Q
ladder and the current physical-Q approximation machinery, while retaining the
elastic run as the zero-loss endpoint. Record full-window relative L2,
correlation, signed amplitude, and phase/lag versus the elastic trace. Require
the attenuation/dispersion difference to decrease consistently with fitted
`tau_s`; freeze any final bound independently of the surface candidate. This
oracle separates rheology convergence from a boundary error because both
viscoelastic and elastic runs use the same surface geometry.

## J. Finite-Q sensitivity control

At least one moderate-Q run must differ materially from both the high-Q and
elastic traces. Reuse the existing SH rheology diagnostics: distance-dependent
amplitude decay, spectral phase/travel-time dispersion, full-waveform relative
L2, and the effective-parameter echo. The control must verify `L>0`, the exact
frequency list, `Q_PARAMETERIZATION_MODE=1`, and distinct `.qs` model hashes.

This condition prevents a false-green run in which parser or routing behavior
silently executes elastically. It is necessary but is not itself evidence of a
correct free surface.

## K. Mechanism coverage

The mandatory finite-Q case uses `L=1`, a 10 Hz relaxation mechanism, and the
current physical-Q mapping with an explicit supported approximation band. The
extended case reuses the verified M4.2.1 physical-Q multi-mechanism fixture:

```text
L=4
FL=(2.7105, 12.2792, 68.1930, 265.2297) Hz
target Qs=30
physical-Q approximation band 5..120 Hz, df=5 Hz
```

An additional `L=3`, `FL=(5,10,20) Hz` sensitivity case may be retained, but
the design and surface recurrence never depend on `L=1` or `tau=2/Qs`.

## L. Pre-fix RED baseline

At the base commit, `FREE_SURF=1` only removes the upper CPML from the
viscoelastic kernels. No active viscoelastic SH traction completion exists.
The future runtime gate must therefore fail the image-source waveform,
high-order boundary residual, or parity contract against this unchanged
solver.

As in M6.1, an omitted-surface negative discriminator must use
`FDORDER>=4`, preferably 12. At FDORDER=2, the persistently zero initialized
`syz[0]` can supply the minimum traction closure even without explicit
high-order ghost completion. FDORDER=2 remains positive mandatory coverage but
is not required to distinguish the legacy state.

Negative controls are:

- current pre-fix `FREE_SURF=1` at FDORDER 12;
- `FREE_SURF=0` with the absorbing top;
- pure/instrumented wrong surface rows `y=h/2` and `y=h`;
- wrong velocity/stress/q parity; and
- a zero-total-stress state with deliberately nonzero surface `q`.

No production modification is needed to construct these controls.

### Q.2 Reference health and frozen numerical limits

Separate real-only and image-only full-space runs prove that both source
contributions are nonzero and temporally separated.  The simultaneous
two-source trace agrees with their independently computed sum at relative L2
`2.36594e-7`.  Integer-cell reference translation gives relative L2
`1.24316e-5`, correlation `0.9999999999227684`, signed amplitude
`1.00000028965`, and zero lag.

The resulting limits, frozen before candidate execution, are:

- primary image waveform: relative L2 at most 0.02, correlation at least
  0.999, signed-amplitude error at most 0.03, and lag at most 0.001 s (two
  samples);
- reference translation: relative L2 at most `5e-5`, correlation at least
  0.999999, and lag at most one sample;
- linear superposition: relative L2 at most `2e-6`;
- `FREE_SURF=0` image-window energy ratio at most 0.10;
- MPI: relative L2 at most `1e-6` and correlation at least 0.999999; and
- hard normalized boundary limits: traction `5e-6`, `D+y vz` `5e-5`, even
  velocity and odd total-stress parity `2e-6`, and `q_l[0]` `2e-6`;
- diagnostic-only full odd-`q` ghost parity, with no acceptance effect.

The translation L2 limit is about four times the measured same-rheology
reference floor.  The primary limits are much wider than both reference floors
but remain well below the observed missing-high-order-closure error.  No CPML
control is subtracted from the primary amplitude oracle.
